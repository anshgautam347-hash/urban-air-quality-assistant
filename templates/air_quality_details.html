Critical Scientific Distinction: Concentration vs. AQI

A frequent misunderstanding is treating raw pollutant concentration (µg/m³) as an AQI value. They are fundamentally different:

Pollutant Concentration (µg/m³)

The physical mass of a chemical contaminant contained within one cubic meter of ambient air. Measured directly by optical particle counters and gas spectrometers.

Air Quality Index (AQI)

A unitless standardized scale (0–500 in the US EPA system) calculated through piecewise linear interpolation to translate disparate chemical masses into an intuitive public health risk indicator.

Mathematical EPA AQI Conversion Formula

The US EPA calculates AQI for any pollutant concentration using piecewise linear interpolation between predefined toxicological breakpoints:

I = [ (I_high - I_low) / (C_high - C_low) ] × (C - C_low) + I_low
